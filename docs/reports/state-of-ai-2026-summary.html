Executive summary

2026 marks a pivot from AI experimentation to enterprise activation. Momentum is real—access, investment, and confidence are rising—but most organizations still capture only a fraction of AI's potential. The gap is not about technology access; it is about usage, scale, work redesign, governance, and strategic reinvention.

Six focus areas

Deloitte recommends six interconnected actions to capture AI's untapped edge:

Figure 6 — Six focus areas to capture AI's untapped edge

  1. Close the access–activation gap — Grassroots adoption plus executive sponsorship; design for deployment from day one.
  2. Unlock human advantage — Redesign work, roles, and career paths holistically.
  3. Build governance before scale — Integrate oversight with existing risk structures.
  4. Address sovereign AI — Map data residency, compute location, and cross-border rules.
  5. Build living tech and data infrastructure — Modular, cloud-native backbone for agentic and physical AI.
  6. Pursue strategic reinvention — Treat AI as foundational to how the organization operates and competes.

Conclusion

The central thesis is "the untapped edge": AI's transformational potential is real and accelerating, but value is constrained by activation, not ambition.

What is working

  • Broader tool access (~60% of workers)
  • Rising investment (84% increasing spend)
  • Growing confidence (78%)
  • Early productivity wins (66%)
  • Clear pathway to production scale

What is not working

  • Low daily usage despite access
  • Pilot-to-production friction
  • Efficiency over reinvention
  • Fluency without work redesign (84%)
  • Governance lagging agents (21% mature)
  • Talent readiness trailing strategy

The strategic divide

A widening gap separates organizations using AI for incremental efficiency from those embedding it into core operations. Winners will move from experimentation to operationalization, redesign human-AI collaboration, establish agent governance early, treat sovereign AI as competitive strategy, modernize infrastructure, and pursue reinvention across multiple horizons.

Bottom line

2026 is less about whether to adopt AI and more about whether organizations can activate it at scale—with the right work design, governance, infrastructure, and strategic intent. The technology edge is available; the competitive edge goes to those who turn access into adoption, pilots into production, and efficiency into enduring differentiation.
